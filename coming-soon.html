What’s coming

Coming soon, and coming later

Rim Reader is in testing on Android. Here is what is still to come, in plain terms and without dates we can’t promise.

The Android app Coming soon to Google Play

Rim Reader runs on Android 10 or newer, on a phone with a gyroscope. It is being tested now and will be released on Google Play. Pricing will be announced at launch.

Bracket print files At launch

The print files for the bracket and all sixteen wedges will be published when the app launches, through a 3D-model site linked from the app and from this site. Until then, you can make your own bracket.

iPhone Coming later

An iPhone version will follow the Android release. It has to pass the same tests as the Android app before it is released.

Shop plans Coming

For mobile mechanics and shops: your name and logo on every report, customer, plate and mileage on each vehicle, before-and-after reports side by side, and a check-only report with a recommended service. These features are built and will come with the shop plans.

Factory specs

Built-in factory alignment specs: later

A later version will look up your vehicle’s factory alignment specs for you: toe, camber, cross camber, caster and thrust limits for your year, make, model and trim, with no typing.

Until then, type in your vehicle’s specs from its service information or a shop’s alignment printout, or use the app’s generic targets for a typical street car, which are always labelled as generic. Entering specs

Why aren’t they built in yet?

Factory alignment data is compiled by specialist companies and licensed to apps. A licence costs thousands of dollars a year, plus a fee for each vehicle looked up and a server to deliver it. That is a significant investment for a new app, so we want to see how drivers and mechanics take to Rim Reader first. If the app finds its users, we plan to add them.

The generic targets are deliberately tighter than most factory tolerances, and were checked against published factory figures for common cars. Where your vehicle’s own specs differ, use those. Owner’s manuals usually don’t list them.

Scanning the VIN fills in the year, make and model today. It identifies the vehicle; it doesn’t give alignment specs.
