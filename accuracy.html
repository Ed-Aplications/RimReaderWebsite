Accuracy and limits

What we have measured, and what we haven’t yet

We publish the numbers from our own tests and how we got them. Repeatable is not the same as right: Rim Reader has not yet been compared with a professional alignment rack. That comparison is planned, and its results will go on this page.

Toe, measured again and again on one car

0.013°rear total toe, lap to lap
0.026°front total toe, lap to lap
0.030°steer-ahead, lap to lap
1 in 8laps needed a reading retaken

Eight full laps in a row on 28 September 2026, about two minutes each, on one car that was not moved, touched or steered between laps. Standard deviation: about two thirds of results fall within that amount of the average. Each front wheel’s own toe scattered 0.037° (front left) and 0.028° (front right). The app’s own pass mark was 0.05° per axle; both axles passed.

On a second car on a different day, front total toe measured four different ways (toe with camber and thrust, toe with thrust, toe only, and the front axle alone) stayed within 0.034°. The front-only lap took 49 seconds.

The conditionsA Google Pixel 8 Pro, one printed bracket held by hand, and one experienced user: the developer. No first-time user has been tested yet. The two-hand hold the app teaches now was refined on 8 October 2026 and hasn’t been re-measured against these numbers.

 Lap-to-lap standard deviation, same morning, car, bracket and phone: 3 closed one-hand laps against the first 5 two-hand laps. With one hand, 2 of 5 laps failed.

Prongs on the metal, not the rubber

In one test session, letting the prongs touch the tire’s rubber roughly doubled the lift-and-replace error (0.017° against 0.009°). Small samples on one day, but the lesson is clear: slide in only until the prong noses meet the lip.

Putting the bracket back on the same spot

Lifting the bracket off a wheel and setting it back moved the reading by typically about 0.022° (standard deviation). That is about 0.08 mm of difference across the 196 mm between the prongs, which is why the app reads every wheel twice.

Camber

0.018°one wheel, 11 separate sessions
0.064°the same readings from gravity alone

Reading the top of the rim as a reference made camber about 3.5 times more repeatable than gravity alone, which is how a simple digital angle gauge reads it. Four wheels with the floor correction took 2½ minutes.

The floor is the weak point. The floor slope at one spot, measured 11 times, repeated only to 0.10°: grit and where the bracket sits matter. On a driveway the two sides of one axle sloped about 1° differently. Choose the smoothest, flattest floor and wipe it.

The phone

Walking round a room and back to the same spot, the phone’s heading came back within 0.001°, 0.009° and 0.039° on three walks. A phone that is still warming up or cooling down drifts more, so let it settle to the outdoor temperature for 3 to 5 minutes before a lap.

The app uses about 2 % of the battery per 15 minutes of measuring, and opens in about a quarter of a second on our test phone.

Not tested yet

  1. No comparison with an alignment rack. Everything above is repeatability: the app agreeing with itself. Whether it agrees with a calibrated rack is the next test. Until then, treat the numbers as estimates and have a shop check before highway driving.
  2. A possible offset is unchecked. Three cars measured on one day all read some front toe-out. That may be the cars, or it may be something the rack comparison will show.
  3. One phone, one bracket, one user, a few cars. Other phones are graded by their own calibration, but no list of tested phones exists yet.
  4. The thrust angle’s repeatability hasn’t been reported yet. A door that hangs differently from its twin could add about 0.1°, which is why thrust within 0.10° reads as straight.
  5. Day-to-day calibration. The angle between the bracket’s two edges moved about 0.03° from one day to the next. That is one reason for the 30-day calibration reminder.
  6. Some rims read poorly. On one car the bracket wouldn’t seat the same way twice at some wheels, and most laps wouldn’t close. Rims with painted, domed or chamfered lips haven’t been tested.
  7. Temperature. Laps haven’t been tested in the cold or the heat yet.

About the numbers on screen

The app shows angles to 0.01°. That is the display, not a claim of 0.01° accuracy: it is finer than the repeatability above. The confidence rating, High, Medium or Low with its reasons, is the better guide to how much a result can be trusted. “A Low result is not necessarily wrong, but it is not evidence.”

What comes next

The plan is to measure cars right before or after a professional alignment rack, five times each, and compare toe, thrust and camber. Then the same tests on more phones. The results, good or bad, will be published here. What’s coming.
