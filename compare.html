Ways to check alignment

Every method has a trade-off

People have aligned cars at home with strings, tape measures and bubble gauges for decades. Here is how the common methods compare, and where Rim Reader fits.

Where Rim Reader differs

  • Against strings and toe plates: no box to square and no tape to read. Like strings, it gives each wheel’s toe against the rear axle, and steer-ahead, which toe plates can’t. It does need a calibrated phone and bracket.
  • Against a plain camber gauge: camber is read against the wheel’s own direction, which made it about 3.5 times more repeatable in our tests, and it measures the floor’s slope beside each tire instead of assuming the floor is level.
  • Against other phone gauges: every wheel is read twice with a lift between, each axle’s drift is measured from repeat visits rather than assumed, the bracket and phone are calibrated together, and the thrust angle is read against the car’s body.
  • Against a shop rack: a rack is the reference, measures caster and more, and gives a live readout. Rim Reader is for checking and adjusting at home, and it tells you to have the alignment checked by a shop before highway driving.

Which is right for you

If you only need to check total toe once after a tie-rod change, a tape and a helper may be enough. If you want each wheel’s toe, steer-ahead and thrust without setting up strings, and you will measure more than once, that is what Rim Reader is for. After any suspension or steering work, and before highway driving or carrying heavy loads, have a qualified shop check the alignment.
