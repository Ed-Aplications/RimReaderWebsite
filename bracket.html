The bracket

A printed bracket that holds your phone true

The bracket holds your phone flat against the wheel, the same way every time, so the app can read the wheel’s angle from the phone’s own sensors.

Our bracket Print files coming soon

The Rim Reader bracket is the one the app is tuned and tested with. It prints on a home 3D printer, needs nothing bought, and fits a wide range of phones with a set of slide-in wedges.

The print files will be available when the app launches.

Before every use

Check the bracket for cracks, looseness or wear, and don’t use it if you find any. A bracket is printed by you or by others, so its quality is in your hands.

Make your own

Seven things a bracket needs

Rim Reader works with a bracket you make yourself if it does these seven things. Then run Bracket Calibration with your phone in it.

  1. Two straight edges, two prongs each. Each long edge has two prongs that rest on the rim’s lip. Ours are 196 mm apart. Prongs closer together work, but dirt under one costs more.
  2. Prongs that reach past the tire. The prongs stick out about 32 mm past the edge, so they reach the lip without touching the tire.
  3. Phone flat, long side along the prongs. The phone lies flat, screen up, its long side along the line between the prongs and its top toward FRONT.
  4. Phone held tight, bracket stiff. The phone is pushed into a corner and clamped, so it cannot shift or rock. Pressing on the far edge should not tilt the phone; ours is a solid 15 mm slab.
  5. Flat back, steady on its end. It lies still on the floor, and stands steady on its end against a wall. The calibration and the floor readings need both.
  6. A FRONT mark. FRONT points to the front of the car at every wheel. The edge on the phone’s right side is the rail edge; camber is read on it.
  7. Run Bracket Calibration. It measures your bracket’s own small differences and refuses a phone that moves. Run it again for a new phone or a new bracket.

Why prong spacing doesn’t need a setting

The app reads the angle of the line between the two prongs straight from the gyroscope, and that angle is the same however far apart the prongs are. A different spacing only changes how much a speck of dirt under a prong costs: 1 mm on 196 mm is 0.29°; on 150 mm, 0.38°.
